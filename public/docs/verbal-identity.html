1 · Tagline lock

The Honest Hair Company.

Locked. Goes everywhere — packaging, website footer, About-page eyebrow, partner pitch deck, founder email signature, Year-1 social bios.

Set in the editorial serif as a wordmark companion: INDITRESS — The Honest Hair Company. Or as a freestanding line in body sans, lowercase or title-case, never all-caps.

Treatment rules:

  • Never punctuate as a question or exclamation. The line is a declaration.
  • Never compress to "Honest Hair Co." in any treatment.
  • Never translate. Stays in English across markets.
  • Always pair with the wordmark in primary brand surfaces (packaging hero, website header, business card). Can stand alone in repeat surfaces (footer, email signature, social caption).

2 · Brand promise (the strapline that proves the tagline)

Factory-direct. Batch-level transparency. Made for the professional.

Locked v3 (2026-04-30). The tagline is the position. The brand promise is the operational claim — three beats that say what we are, what we do, who it is for. Each beat is non-negotiable:

  • Factory-direct — we are not a trader. The hair is sourced, processed, finished, and shipped from the same address. (File 21 non-negotiable #1: "We are not a trader. We are a factory.")
  • Batch-level transparency — every shipment carries a Batch ID linked to a five-dimension disclosure page (see ITS™, file 20). The buyer reads what is in the bundle before they sample, before they buy, and after delivery.
  • Made for the professional — the brand is built for the person whose reputation depends on the hair behaving the same way next month as it did this month.

The hero is the brand promise expanded. The home-page hero in §6.1 takes these three beats and inserts the five-dimension visual expansion under beat 2 — Origin · Cuticle · Processing · Performance · Supply. That structural relationship is the spine of the brand's first surface.

Version notes: - v1 promise was Verified by the maker. Approved by the expert. — beautiful, but upstream-coded; the buyer was implicit. Retired. - v2 promise was Factory-direct. Disclosed batch by batch. Made for the professional. — moved to a buyer-facing verb but slightly more closed than the brand wanted. - v3 promise (this one) trades the verb Disclosed for the noun-phrase Batch-level transparency — drawn from Deepak's blue rewrite and the ITS™ framework language. Net: the rhythm stays three-beat parallel, the priority words land (factory-direct · batch-level transparency · professional), and the line reads more naturally for a cold buyer scanning the home page.

This change supersedes file 18 §3.

Section title: Why Indi Tress exists

Eyebrow: Founder's note

Headline: This did not start as a business idea.

Body: About twelve years ago, a simple request to help source Indian hair led to an uncomfortable discovery.

Hair was being mixed, relabelled, and sold under terms that had no consistent meaning. What buyers received depended less on what was promised, and more on what happened behind the scenes.

At that time, most Indian hair — despite being the largest global source — was exported as raw material and processed elsewhere. Very few players were building controlled manufacturing systems at origin.

Indi Tress was built in response to that gap.

Not to market better. But to understand sourcing, processing, and quality at a level where it could be repeated — not guessed.

Over the last decade, we have worked closely with professional buyers, retailers, and brands to develop shades, textures, and quality that hold over time.

We are still learning. And we continue to build systems that make the industry more transparent than we found it.

4 · Voice & tone guide

The voice descriptor (one-line paraphrase, lifted from Deepak's brand book)

Calm authority. Precise. Grounded. Honest. Assured. Never loud. Never exaggerated. Always real.

If a writer can pass everything they write through that line and have it still hold, the writing is in voice. If any of those eight words cracks under the weight of the copy — rewrite.

The voice — Voice B, Confident Founder

Inditress speaks like the founder speaks: declarative, plainspoken, technically literate, and unafraid of silence. The brand says less than its peers, with cleaner language. It refuses warmth-as-performance. It uses specifics where peers reach for adjectives.

On the discovery, Deepak ranked Voice B (Confident Founder) first and Voice D (Warm Human) last. That ranking shapes everything below. Warmth is preserved as a service behaviour — named contacts, founder visits, partners remembered by name — not as a copy register. Anyone writing for Inditress who reaches for warmth-words has missed the brief.

The 7 writing rules

Each rule comes with a one-line example of the rule kept and the rule broken. If a paragraph passes all seven, it sounds like Inditress. If it fails any, rewrite.

Rule 1 — Say what is true. Then stop. - Kept: "Every bundle is finished by hand in Haryana." - Broken: "Every bundle is lovingly finished by hand in our beautiful Haryana facility, where our dedicated craftspeople work tirelessly to bring you the very best."

Rule 2 — Specifics, not adjectives. - Kept: "Graded against a 14-point standard. Verified at origin and after installation." - Broken: "Premium quality, exceptional consistency, unparalleled craftsmanship."

Rule 3 — Voice over warmth. - Kept: "If you sell on price, this is the wrong supplier for you." - Broken: "Hi gorgeous! We'd love to be your hair bestie 💕"

Rule 4 — Trusted, not luxurious. - Kept: "The most trusted source of real Indian hair." - Broken: "An indulgent luxury experience for the discerning connoisseur."

Rule 5 — Tell, don't sell. - Kept: "Apply for partner access." - Broken: "Don't miss out — sign up now and get exclusive access today!"

Rule 6 — No exclamation marks. No discount theatre. - Kept: "Stylist-trade pricing applies on verified accounts." - Broken: "MEGA SALE!! 30% off everything this weekend only!!"

Rule 7 — The first person plural is the founder, not a marketing team. - Kept: "We do not work with anyone whose first question is price." - Broken: "We at Inditress are passionate about empowering hair professionals everywhere."

The Inditress sentence-shape — Observation → Truth → Conclusion

Lifted from the Advanced Brand Book PDF (Deepak, file 22). This is the canonical sentence shape for the brand. Every Inditress claim moves through three beats:

  1. Observation — what is empirically the case.
  2. Truth — what that empirical case actually means.
  3. Conclusion — what follows for the buyer.

Canonical example (Deepak's words):

Anyone can deliver a good first batch. Very few can deliver the second.

That sentence is the cleanest articulation of the entire brand position in two beats. It earns placement as the wall-quote on the Proof & Consistency page (see file 21 §3) and as a callout in the founder film.

The final principle (the test before publishing)

If it feels like marketing, remove it. If it feels real, keep it. — Deepak, Advanced Brand Book PDF.

Tone calibration by surface

Surface Voice register Density Posture
Home page hero Voice B 5 to 12 words Declarative
About / Story Voice B Long-form prose First-person founder
Transparency Standard page Voice A → Voice B Specs + framing prose Working document
Product description Voice A (Clinical Technical) 80–120 words Specs + provenance
Partner application page Voice B Plainspoken · directive "If x, this. If not, that."
Email confirmations Voice B Concise · named-partner First-name greeting · what's done · what's next
Founder film script Voice B First-person · unhurried Founder speaking to a peer
Social caption Voice B 1–2 sentences Statement, not invitation
Customer service reply Voice B Concise · honest Names what was wrong, says what's next

Voice A — the secondary register

Voice A (Clinical Technical) handles QC reports, sourcing data, process descriptions, batch certifications, and the technical part of product copy. It is more measured, more taxonomic, and uses the vocabulary of the manufacturing floor. Voice B leads the brand. Voice A backs it up.

Voice A example (product spec block):

Bundle 7B-2026-04. Cuticle-aligned. Single-donor verified at intake. Length: 18". Texture: 2A natural. Weight: 100g. Origin: Tirumala temple, Andhra Pradesh. Processed: 14-day acid-free wash, hand-graded, hand-wefted. QC inspected: Priya M., 2026-04-22. ITS™-v1 → Batch ID linked.

5 · Anti-vocabulary + preferred-vocabulary lists

The anti-vocabulary (banned across every Inditress surface)

  • Bellami-coded: Buss Down · Baddie Bundles · Ten10s · slay · queen · drip · iconic.
  • Generic-luxe: opulent · indulgent · pamper · pampering · luxurious · pristine · exquisite · sumptuous.
  • Marketing slop: game-changer · revolutionary · amazing · incredible · best-in-class · unparalleled · curated · elevated · journey · ecosystem · solution.
  • Warmth-as-performance: bestie · sis · gorgeous · darling · loves · babes · honey.
  • Hype trigger words: don't miss out · limited time · while supplies last · act now · transform your look.

The preferred vocabulary (the words the brand reaches for)

  • Process words: verified · disclosed · cuticle-aligned · sourced · made · finished · matched · aligned · graded · traced · inspected · approved · documented.
  • Posture words: honest · consistent · proven · grounded · raw · calm · precise · assured · accountable.
  • Relationship words: partner · maker · expert · stylist · salon · wholesaler · friend.
  • Specifics: batch · origin · factory · standard · single-donor · 14-point · cuticle · weft · root · tip · ITS™ · five dimensions.

If a copywriter is reaching for a marketing slop word, the right move is to replace it with a specific from the preferred list. "Game-changing quality" becomes "Cuticle-aligned, single-donor, graded against a 14-point standard."

6.4 · How We Work page (rebuilt per file 21)

Replaces the v1 How we work with you page. Now structured around Deepak's four-step partnership flow, with the v1 service-standard reframing folded in at the close.

Page heading:

How partnerships actually start.

Key line (lock):

We would rather lose a large order than win the wrong partnership.

The four steps:

  1. Conversation. Tell us what you are sourcing for and what has gone wrong with previous suppliers. We answer in writing within 24 working hours.
  2. Sample. A single bundle, batch-disclosed against the five dimensions of the Inditress Transparency Standard. Test it on your own client.
  3. Small order. The first 2–3 orders are a learning and alignment phase, not a volume phase. We keep them small on purpose. This reduces risk on both sides.
  4. Scale only after consistency. Volume conversations begin only once the second batch has matched the first. Not before.

Service-standard framing (kept from v1):

Inditress works with partners on the partner's schedule. We answer thoroughly, not instantly.

We reply to every email within 24 working hours, often sooner. We use email and chat — not phone calls — because the questions our partners send us deserve a written record, an attached image, or a referenced batch number.

If you are a verified partner, your account is assigned a named contact at Inditress. They reply with their own name, signed personally.

If you need an urgent answer — write urgent in the subject line. We see it.

CTA at page close:

If transparency matters to you, let's start with a conversation.
