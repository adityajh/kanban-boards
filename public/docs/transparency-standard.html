Verbatim capture — The Indi Tress Transparency Standard™ (ITS™)

Header

The Indi Tress Transparency Standard™ (ITS™) How to read our hair. How to read us.

Why this exists

There is no shared industry standard for clearly differentiating good hair from inconsistent hair. As a result, buyers often rely on labels, promises, and samples that may not fully reflect future shipments.

The Inditress Transparency Standard (ITS™) exists to reduce ambiguity. It defines:

  • How we classify hair
  • What we disclose
  • How we ensure consistency
  • How each batch is documented

This is not a marketing page. It is a working document.

What this standard is — and is not

ITS™ is:

  • A clear explanation of how we define and process hair
  • A framework for batch-level traceability
  • A structure for accountability
  • A reference point before sampling or ordering

ITS™ is not:

  • A guarantee that all hair behaves identically
  • A replacement for real-world testing
  • A claim of superiority

It is simply how we work — disclosed openly.

Our non-negotiables (the six "we will never")

We state these publicly because they define our internal acceptance criteria.

  1. We will never mix dyed hair with virgin hair.
  2. We will never mix non-Remy hair with Remy hair.
  3. We will never mix human hair with synthetic or animal fibres.
  4. We will never ship ratios, sizes, or weights that differ from agreed standards.
  5. We will never ship shades that do not match the approved shade reference.
  6. We will never ship any batch that does not pass internal QC.

If it does not pass internally, it does not ship.

How we ensure consistency

Consistency is not achieved at the end. It is designed into the process.

Our systems include:

  • Documented SOPs for each processing stage
  • Weekly SOP training for factory staff
  • In-line quality checks during processing
  • Final QC on both raw material and finished goods

Early orders are intentionally kept small. The first 2–3 orders are treated as a learning and alignment phase, not a volume phase. This reduces risk on both sides.

Behaviour proof: the pre-lightened strand

In select cases, we include one pre-lightened strand from the same batch as the supplied hair.

This strand is not for shade matching. It is a behaviour proof.

Only genuinely virgin, high-quality hair can lift evenly to light tones without patchiness, excessive breakage, or unpredictable undertones. By showing how one strand behaves, we demonstrate how the rest of the batch is expected to behave under the same conditions.

Batch disclosure (rolling out)

We are introducing batch-level disclosure to increase accountability and traceability. Each batch under ITS™ is linked to a unique Batch ID. When scanned via QR, it leads directly to a batch-specific disclosure page.

No homepage redirects. No marketing overlays.

Each batch page includes:

  • Origin and collection method
  • Cuticle direction disclosure
  • Processing details
  • QC checkpoints passed
  • Lightening behaviour proof (if applicable)
  • Supply chain transparency

If a batch performs well, the absence of complaints is also recorded — traceability replaces argument.

This system is being rolled out progressively and will evolve with buyer feedback.

If something goes wrong

Quality issues are addressed through a clear, documented process.

For a replacement claim:

  • The batch reference must be shared
  • Photos and/or videos are required
  • Claims must be raised within 7 days of delivery

For verified quality issues, replacement is arranged. For preference-based differences (feel, dryness, packing style), we stay involved to explain and align expectations — even when replacement is not applicable.

How to use this standard as a buyer

We recommend:

  1. Read this page fully
  2. Test samples yourself
  3. Start with small orders
  4. Observe consistency over time
  5. Build forward only after alignment

This is how most long-term relationships with us begin.

How we define hair quality (plain language, no marketing claims)

The hair industry uses terms such as raw, virgin, Remy, donor, and double drawn without a universal technical standard. Below is how we define them based on over a decade of sourcing and manufacturing experience. The following definitions are used under the Indi Tress Transparency Standard (ITS™). These definitions are descriptive — not promotional.

Raw Hair

Raw hair refers to hair in its natural state immediately after collection. It means the hair has not been altered or further worked upon after being collected.

In practice, raw hair can vary significantly depending on the method of collection. Raw hair may come from:

  • Temple donations (tonsured hair)
  • Individual ponytail donations or sales
  • Salon cuttings
  • Collected hair — hair gathered after combing (such as hair balls)

Each source can produce hair with different characteristics in terms of alignment, colour, and overall consistency. Raw status describes the condition of the hair after collection — not its quality level.

Hair Origin

Hair origin refers to the country where the raw hair was originally collected — not where it was later processed or marketed.

  • India is widely recognized as the largest global source of raw human hair, with a significant portion originating through temple donations.
  • China is one of the largest processors and exporters of finished human hair products.
  • Other countries may supply hair in smaller quantities, typically through individual ponytail donations or salon collection.

In the global market, origin names such as "Brazilian," "Peruvian," or similar descriptors are often used as commercial labels. In many cases, these terms may refer to marketing positioning rather than the actual country of collection.

Because hair is frequently exported and relabelled, buyers should request clarity on:

  • Country of collection
  • Method of collection
  • Country of processing

Origin transparency helps reduce misunderstanding and sets realistic expectations regarding supply consistency.

Donor Hair

Donor hair refers to hair collected directly from an individual donor. This may occur through:

  • Ponytail cutting
  • Temple tonsuring

If collected and handled correctly, donor hair typically maintains natural cuticle alignment (one-directional alignment).

However, donor hair is not automatically virgin hair. The donor's hair may have been untreated natural colour, dyed, henna-treated, or grey. Donor status describes collection method — not chemical history.

Collected Hair

Collected hair is not obtained directly from a single donor. Common sources include:

  • Salon cuttings
  • Hair gathered after combing
  • Secondary mixed collection sources

Collected hair is often mixed in: cuticle direction, chemical history, colour composition. This type of hair often requires additional processing to achieve alignment and uniformity.

Cuticle Alignment

Cuticle alignment means that hair cuticles are oriented in a single direction (root-to-tip consistency). This may occur:

  • Naturally — in the case of donor hair, when hair is cut as a single ponytail
  • Mechanically — when collected hair is aligned through processing equipment

When alignment is natural, cuticles are typically intact. When alignment is mechanical, 100% cuticle alignment is difficult to achieve. In such cases, manufacturers may remove cuticles through processing to reduce tangling. Cuticle alignment describes direction of cuticles — not overall quality.

Virgin Hair vs Processed Hair

Virgin hair refers to hair that has not undergone chemical treatment such as dyeing, bleaching, perming, or relaxing. Virgin hair may originate from donor hair (most common) or carefully sorted collected hair (limited availability).

Processed hair refers to hair that has been chemically altered from its original state. This may include dyeing, bleaching, acid processing, or cuticle removal. Processing type affects behaviour, longevity, and stability.

Single Drawn vs Double Drawn

Single Drawn reflects natural distribution of hair strand lengths within a ponytail.

  • Hair is aligned from the root
  • Natural shorter strands remain within the bundle
  • The thickness gradually tapers toward the ends, resembling natural hair growth

Most donor hair is single drawn by default because it maintains the original strand distribution of the donor.

Double Drawn is sorted and aligned from both root and tip.

  • Different lengths of strands are removed and redistributed
  • The bundle appears thicker and fuller toward the ends

Double drawn hair is often produced using collected hair by sorting individual strands by length, grouping similar lengths together, and recombining them to create specific fullness levels or commercial grades like 8A, 10A, etc.

A fuller appearance does not automatically indicate higher overall quality. Fullness should be evaluated alongside other parameters such as hair origin, cuticle direction, processing history, etc.

The ITS™ Mark — what it is

Each ITS™ product carries: ITS™-v1 → Batch ID: [Unique Code]

This mark indicates:

  • One defined batch
  • QR-linked disclosure
  • Internal QC passed
  • Traceability for any quality concern

It does not claim superiority. It claims traceability and disclosure.

QR destination behaviour

Each QR leads directly to a batch-specific disclosure page. No homepage redirects. No promotional overlays.

The batch record includes five disclosure dimensions:

⭐ The 5 Dimensions of Transparency

Every hair lot is classified across five dimensions. These are the operational definition of "Honest Hair Company." Use this framework wherever the brand needs to prove the claim — hero, packaging, QR keepsake, About page, partner pitch.

1. Origin Transparency

  • Country of origin
  • Method of collection — temple / salon / combed hair

2. Cuticle & Direction Integrity

  • Cuticle present / partial / removed
  • Natural or mechanical alignment

3. Processing Disclosure

  • Chemical processing (Yes / No)
  • Silicone or coating status (Yes / No)
  • Colour processing status — Virgin / Dyed / Bleached

4. Performance Proof

  • Pre-lightened strand present (Yes / No)

5. Supply Chain Visibility

  • Factory direct or intermediary
  • Batch-coded traceability (Yes / No)
